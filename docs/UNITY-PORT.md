## How the port works
**The JS game is the spec, and the port is proven by replaying it.** Nothing is "retuned by eye" in Unity until it matches. *(Since D124 this holds for behaviour; the look is Unity's own, and its intentional differences are listed under "Rendering differences" below.)*

- **Pure C# simulation, no Unity physics.** A line-by-line port of `hero.js`, `stage.js`, `dummy.js` and `striker.js` into an assembly with no Unity
  dependencies, using `double` so the numbers match. No Rigidbody2D, no colliders for the hero: his own AABB collision, exactly as now. It is stepped at a fixed 60 Hz
  by an accumulator, with rendering blended between steps on fast displays (as now).
- **The numbers live in one place:** a `KanameTuning` ScriptableObject that mirrors the `K` table, so the designer can tune in the Inspector. Units stay Little Shinobi's
  pixels (a tile is 32 u; Kaname is 16 × 34 u); one Unity unit is one tile.
- **Input:** Unity's Input System, actions mirroring the current bindings (move, up, down, jump, dash, attack, parry), keyboard and gamepad (PlayStation pad through the standard gamepad layout).
- **Hit-stop and the zoom kick** are done in the simulation (a freeze that keeps buffered presses), never with `Time.timeScale`. The camera is a critically damped spring
  (Unity's `SmoothDamp` is the same maths) leading him by 36 u.
- **The look is drawn by a view layer** (`KanameView`) that reads the simulation's state (`vis`, `swing`, `trail`) and poses child objects: the head, body, feet and sash as
  sprites; the scarf as a tapered line along the same 8-point chain; the blade, its crescent trail, the speed lines and the glints as small generated meshes; the glow as
  soft sprites or URP 2D bloom. The pose maths (`kanamePose`, `kanameHeadFrame`) is ported as it is, so the scarf stays tied to his head.
- **Real art comes later and slots in:** Unity's 2D Animation and PSD Importer packages take layered Photoshop files (`.psb`) and rig them, so the Adobe tools the
  designer mentioned feed straight in. The procedural silhouette is the placeholder until then.
- **Tests:** Unity Test Framework. The 82 JavaScript acceptance tests become EditMode tests on the simulation. On top of that, **golden traces**: a script runs scripted
  inputs in the JS game (deterministic, seeded) and records the state every step; the C# replays the same inputs and must match to a small tolerance. If the traces
  match, the feel matches. PlayMode tests cover drawing and take the evidence screenshots for a contact sheet.

## Rendering differences from the JavaScript game, on purpose (D124)
Since D124 the JavaScript game is the reference for behaviour and Unity owns the look, so the Unity drawing may now differ from
`js/art_hero.js`. Every intentional difference is listed here in the same change that makes it.
- **The forward, up and down cuts have five angles each and read the swing's own timing (task 1, D123, revision 1),** not one arc eased over 0.19 s. The blade's angle in the world is **C** (cocked) at the press; it accelerates in (`C + (I - C)u^2`, u = T / 0.03 s), passing **N** (where the cut enters the box: the trail starts here), to **I** on the first step the box is live (T = 0.03 s: mid-box, the picture a hit freezes, since a blow lands on that step); it sweeps from I to **O** over `HeroLook.SweepTime` = 0.08 s (a look constant, not a tuning number; eased out), is held at O until the blade stops being live (0.14 s), then eases from O to **E** by the end of the swing (0.2 s) while the existing fade runs. Radians, 0 straight ahead and positive down, tip radius 45 as before. Final values: **forward, high to low**, pivot (11, -15): C -1.50, N -0.68, I -0.22, O 0.19, E 0.20; **forward, low to high** (the `Alt` cut, an arc of its own and not the first one reversed), pivot (11, -15): 0.09, 0.09, -0.30, -0.76, -1.05; **up**, pivot (14, -22): -2.85, -2.60, -1.80, -1.22, -1.00; **down**, pivot (12, -10): 0.80, 1.20, 1.75, 2.45, 2.60. (Revision 1's table had the high-to-low O at 0.18 and the low-to-high C and N at 0.12; the tests moved them: see the log.) The JavaScript drawing keeps its old arcs (forward ±1.15 rad, up -2.5 to -0.64, down 0.64 to 2.5, one easing over 0.112 s, which ran from 16 u above his head to 27 u under the floor). The first two steps show only the cocked blade, no trail. `LookAtk` and `SwingState` carry `Live0` and `T` for this; `PoseReel`'s own swing gives `Live0` = 0.03. **The draw and the guard keep their arcs and their easing.**
- **The arcs are pinned to the world, not to the lean:** the body's current lean is taken off the blade's and the trail's angle for forward, up and down cuts (`HeroLook.Pinned`), so leaning into a run or a sprint moves his hand but does not tilt the cut. The hand still goes to the pivot in his own frame and moves with him.
- **The slash of a forward, up or down cut has two layers (task 2, D123, D127),** drawn about the swing's pivot with the lean taken off, as filled polygons only (no strokes, no halo), by `KanameArt.DrawSlash`. The old violet-black bands (`28,12,64` at 0.5 and 0.45, `18,6,44` at 0.4), the glow band outside the tip's path and the stroked pale edge are gone for these cuts (the draw keeps its old trail, U5; the guard has none).
  - **Timing: both layers exist only while the blade can hit** (`Live0` 0.03 s <= T < `Live1` 0.14 s; `SweepEnd` = 0.11 s). Nothing before the box goes live (steps 0-1), nothing from step 9 on (T = 0.15 s: the follow-through), nothing after an early end (a dash after a hit takes the swing out of the look). Hit-stop repeats the hit step's picture.
  - **Every angle of the slash is the blade tip's, not its axis's** (task 2, revision 2): the curved blade's tip trails its axis by `lag` = atan2(`BladeLen` x 0.1, 5 + `BladeLen`) = 0.0886 rad (about 4 u at R, the same lag task 1's trail used), so the cut runs from N + lag to O + lag, the bright part ends at the blade's angle + lag (capped at O + lag), the afterimage spans tail + lag to head + lag clamped to (I..O) + lag, and the marks sit there. (The first build drew the axis angles, so the cut ran about 4 u past anywhere the tip goes.)
  - **The cut (inner), D127:** from the first live step it covers the whole damaging sweep **N to O** (as the tip's angles), its outer edge the tip's path (radius R = 45), pointed at both ends and never beyond R. ## Decisions
1. **A git checkpoint** of the JavaScript prototype: **yes, done** (one local commit and a tag, nothing pushed).

## Risks and fallbacks
- The Unity CLI is in beta (1.0.0-beta.13) and the official plugin is new (September 2026). *Fallback:* Unity Hub for installing, and Coplay's MCP for Unity for driving the Editor.
- A Unity ID, its terms and the Personal license are the designer's to enter. Nothing can proceed without that step.
- The 5 GB download takes a while on a slow connection.
- **The procedural ink art is the biggest visual job** to rebuild (about 300 lines of drawing code). It is a placeholder for hand-made art anyway.
- Floating-point differences between JavaScript and C#: the simulation uses `double` and the traces allow a tolerance of 0.001 u.
- Unity 6.0 LTS support ends this month: we deliberately start on 6.3.
