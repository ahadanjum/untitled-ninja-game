# Kaname

His name, 要, means the pin that holds a folding fan together. He is a young shinobi of about sixteen. He wears a classical blue outfit, with a mask over his face, bare arms, and a dark purple band round his head whose tail streams behind him. On the surface he is restrained and dutiful. Underneath, humour is how he copes.

## Chapter 1: The Night of the Pact
It is dusk at the clan's compound on the mountain. Word has come that the Severing, a rival clan, are raiding, and the council and most of the clan's fighters ride out to meet them. Before they go, the dogs won't settle. The council seals the sanctum, a forbidden hall in the oldest part of the compound where a cursed blade is kept. They bind its door with rope and seals and take the key.

Kaname stays behind with the old, the young, the injured and a few guards. In the safe room are his mother, his younger sibling and the Elder. The Elder is the clan's godfather, once an excellent shinobi and now a father to Kaname. He is the calmest one there. His mother hums the lullaby she sang to Kaname as a child to settle his frightened sibling.

The attack comes. To the people in the compound it looks like an assault out of the forest. Demons pour in, and they walk past easier prey to get to Kaname. He promises his mother he will hold the gate, and he meets the first demon with a dry joke.

As things worsen, the Elder gives him a rope. "Whatever you choose, you choose it for them. Remember that." His mother presses a key into his hand, a spare for the sanctum, a custom the Elder taught her. She tells him to use it if the clan falls. The forbidden hall, he finds, has been swept and kept tidy.

At the sanctum, Kaname makes a pact with the blade to save his family and the clan. Paper seals bind it, and its glow is a deep violet-black. The curse scars his right forearm, and it is the only arm that can hold the blade. When he tries to throw it away, it returns to its sheath. The first time he takes a dying demon's power into himself, he is visibly repelled by it.

He fights through the compound, past a stronger demon and then the one leading the assault. In the last fight the blade's hunger moves his arm, and for a moment he is not the one swinging. The compound survives.

At dawn the council returns, too soon. The clan's law is that a shinobi who bargains with demons is cast out. His father, a council elder, asks first about the blade, and he alone does not flinch at the arm. His mother says nothing. The Elder protests the sentence. His sibling, who has always been on his side, flinches once, then reaches for him at once, ashamed. That flinch is what decides it. Kaname chooses to go, because his family is safer without him. The clan sets him no terms.

## Chapter 2: The Road of Exile
Years pass, about four. Kaname's goal is entirely his own. He will defeat the worst demons of the exile lands and master the curse, so the clan has to take him back.

The Road runs through the exile lands. At its centre is a crossroads, with a rival clan's land on each side of it. Demons still walk past easier prey to reach him, and one uses a technique he recognises from his own clan's training. At every place he rests, he remembers the Elder teaching him, and someone has left a paper crane there, a guardian's quiet care. Somewhere on the road he hears the lullaby hummed.

Ordinary people see a threat. A ferryman, a widow, a child and a healer flinch, though they are kind in small ways. Each demon he kills leaves him more resigned than repelled. He covers it with jokes, and sometimes a joke doesn't land, and there is a silence afterwards.

The three clans each hunt him:
- **The Severing**: "What cannot be made right must be cut away." They are ascetic, merciless and fair. Their leader cut off her own cursed arm, and she understands his better than he does. She tells him: "The demons are not born. They are made."
- **The Wardens**: "What cannot be cut must be kept." They are patient and bureaucratic. They seal the cursed and vault them. Their Keeper is ancient and holds a thousand seals. The Keeper tells him: "Your clan keeps a patron, and the patron is older than your clan." Of the seal tags on his blade, with the clan's mark and a Warden's script beneath: "Those are our seals. Who taught your clan our seals?"
- **The Open Hand**: "What cannot be cut or kept must be carried, and the carrier must answer for it." They are healers, traders and hired blades who shelter the cursed for a price. Their leader is a shrewd broker, compromised and guilty. The broker tells him: "You are not a weapon. You are a body."

Each despises the others. The Severing call the Wardens cowards who feed a sickness in a cage, and the Open Hand mercenaries who sell the leash. The Wardens call the Severing butchers who release what they kill, and the Open Hand people who let the thing walk. The Open Hand say the Severing kill children for being born, and the Wardens are jailers who call it duty. Kaname can't make sense of any of the riddles yet. In each land he also finds a hidden way that leads toward home.

Near the compound he comes across dead rival scouts. The clan calls them raiders, and it feeds the old feud with the Severing. Something is wrong at home, and he knows it.

At the crossroads he faces the greatest demon of the exile lands. It tells him: "Your family did this to you." He takes it for a lie, but news from home pulls him back. He goes early, still an outcast, hoping he has done enough and knowing he hasn't.

## Chapter 3: The Homecoming
The compound's gate is closed to him, so he gets in over its cliffs and rooftops with the Elder's rope, through places that were shut to him on the night of the pact. It is daylight, and the clan is back in power.

A monument says raiders killed his father, in the third year of his exile. In his old room he finds his mother's ledger of tonics he was given as a child, which she kept without knowing what it was, and an unsent letter from his father. The letter says his father knew about the experiments, and that he was wrong. Neither matches the monument.

In the council hall, masked members speak in riddles. They tell Kaname, or let slip, that the clan has been dealing with demons for years. They believe they are forging a weapon, and he is that weapon. The clan raised him, dosed him, and expected the pact. Each time he defeats one of them, a mask comes off.

Beneath the compound are halls he never knew: a cellar, a prison, rooms where people were experimented on, and a demon-gate. In them he finds the Elder under guard. The council kills the Elder in front of him. With his last breath the Elder tells him to accept it, and says his mother and sibling have already been sent through the demon-gate. The council did it for no longer acting in line with the clan: they took his side, his mother gave him the key, and she questioned the tonics. The Elder presses a folded paper crane into his hand: *save them*. Kaname realises the cranes were the Elder's.

Kaname rebels. The council banishes him through the gate as well: "We're sending you there so you can rot with them." He falls.

## Chapter 4: The Descent
Hell is a world of its own, a dark mirror beneath the mountain, with its own music and some small warmth. Its pulls are three. He wants to reach his family. The people there, some caged for years by the clan, treat him as one who got out. And voices tell him the pain will stop if he gives in.

At the bottom, hell's great demon, the Gatekeeper, holds his mother and sibling in two cages over lava. He recognises Kaname, because both of them said yes to save someone. He hums the lullaby, a note off from the way Kaname's mother sang it. Only one cage can be saved, because opening one drops the other.

Kaname refuses to choose. He fights the Gatekeeper and wins, and it changes nothing. The rule of the cages is older than the Gatekeeper. His mother takes the choice from him: "Take your sibling. I'm sorry for what's happened to you. Here's the truth." She lets her own cage fall, which lowers her child's to safety, and she dies. As she goes she tells him what she learned from the caged dissenters. The demons are people, made from children. The clan serves a Patron, a demon whose face no one has seen. The council believes it is making a weapon, but Kaname was made for something else, a purpose she only half understands: to be a body. She does not know who the Patron is.

He climbs out with his sibling. On the way he learns something else: the made want him to take their power, because it ends their pain. The curse he hates is the thing that can free them. Every demon he has killed since the pact looks different to him now.

## Chapter 5: The Other Side
They come up in the Open Hand's land. A healer, a minor member of the clan who breaks its rules, tends to Kaname and his sibling. His sibling, frightened of him at first, is one of the first to accept him.

Each of the three clans tells him the story of how all this began, and each leaves out its own sin. Kaname works out the rest. Centuries ago a family had a child touched by a demon, and three orders each had an answer. The Severing would have killed the child, and their purges killed four children and made the curse jump. The Wardens caged it, and their seals fed the curse and ripened it. The Open Hand hid the family, then gave up where they were when pressed, and in time the clan hired them to hunt Kaname.

Hunted by all three, the parents made a pact with a demon to keep their child alive. The demon kept the bargain, and kept the child. He became the demon's first vessel, failed, and became the Gatekeeper. The family became the clan, and the demon became its patriarch, the godfather who gave it its arts and asked for vessels in return. Each generation the clan made vessels from children, and the failures became the made, the demons. A vessel has to say yes, so the clan arranged every circumstance that might make a child say it. Kaname is the first to be strong enough, and willing enough, to succeed.

His pact and the clan's are the same act for the same reason. He paid with himself, and the clan paid with other people's children. Nobody is innocent, including the clans now asking for his help.

Their lands turn. The Wardens bow after a duel, and the Open Hand's land flips completely. It is acceptance and respect, not forgiveness. People who flinched at him now look at him with hope. One Severing holdout never bows and offers to cut off his cursed arm. The three clans ask him to help restore the balance.

The last fragment names the Patron: the Elder. He is alive, and the death Kaname watched was staged. He is the demon of the founding pact, older than the clan, who has loved the family he built with a possessive love. He also wants more demonic power in the world and the balance changed. Kaname was meant to be his body.

The Elder arranged all of it. The raid was a rumour about the Severing, and the council's absence let him open the demon-gate and perform the rituals that night. The demons came up through the mountain's roots. Kaname's exile, his homecoming and his fall into hell were his design. The council thought they were banishing a traitor, and the Elder made sure Kaname went where he would grow strong enough. His mother and sibling were sent to hell as part of the same plan. His father began to doubt, and the Elder had him killed. The monument lied to keep Kaname hunting rivals.

Looking back, the cranes were never a guardian's care. They were the Elder steering him. The dogs knew, and so did the council when it sealed the sanctum. The hall was swept and the spare key given because the sanctum had been made ready for him. "Whatever you choose, you choose it for them" and "accept it" were the Elder asking for a willing yes. The dead scouts weren't raiders but rivals who came to stop it. Demons walked past easier prey because the Elder sent them. His name, the pin of a folding fan, was the point everything hinged on.

## Chapter 6: The Reckoning
He goes home for three reasons: to restore the balance, to find out whether everything he has been told is true, and because of a guilt he can't shake. He has learned his mentor is a demon who groomed him as a vessel, and he blames himself for his clan's fall, for his own self-hatred and for his mother's death.

The compound has been devoured, and its shinobi are hollowed out. The Elder has used up the council. One or two members are still standing. One is tragic, and one dies helping him. At the sanctum, where it began, the Elder confirms all of it to his face. He is kind, even now: "Stop hating yourself. Let it in." He needs Kaname to say yes. He hums the lullaby to coax it, and it does nothing.

The lullaby was a lock. The clan sang it to every vessel to open the mind to the Patron's voice. Kaname's mother, suspecting, changed one note when she sang it to him. That is why the grooming never fully took, and why he can refuse. His choice was made possible by his mother's small act of love.

In the final fight the Elder wears the council's powers, and everything Kaname has faced comes back at him.

When it ends, the Elder lies at his feet, and the last of the seals is almost burned. His mother's death was a choice taken from him. This one is his. Both options are right, and neither is free.

**Let go.** He kills the Elder. The seals burn out and the pact ends. The curse lifts, the scar fades, and the made rest, their voices going quiet. The blade is plain steel, and he is a shinobi again with only what he was taught. It means peace and loss, and people see a man.

**Carry.** He takes the Elder in, absorbing him into the curse along with his hunger to bring more of his kind into the world. The seals burn on Kaname's terms, with no master and no leash. The chorus of the freed swells, and the Elder's voice stays inside him. He becomes a power no one controls. It means company and vigilance, and people see a hopeful spirit or something to fear. The epilogue holds both.

His sibling, the first to accept him, sees hope in him at the end. In either ending he refuses what the clan made him for, and his acceptance is defiance: he keeps what he is and refuses what he was made to be.
