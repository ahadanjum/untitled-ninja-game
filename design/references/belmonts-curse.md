# Castlevania: Belmont's Curse — reference notes

The designer's art-and-movement touchstone ("the same vibe… how the characters move and function") and the model for the
ninja's traversal tool. Researched 2026-10-17; the game launched **October 15, 2026**, two days earlier, so everything here
rests on launch coverage, not long-term opinion. Summaries, not quotes; nothing here is a source of assets.

## Facts
- **Made by** Evil Empire for Konami, with Motion Twin advising (the Dead Cells team's lineage). Announced February 12, 2026;
  PS5, Xbox Series X|S, PC, Switch. A **free demo of the first chapter** shipped on October 1, 2026: the same shape as our
  demo goal.
- **Art is cel-shaded 3D models rendered to look 2D** (Xbox Wire). Vivid, graphic colour: Paris opening in magenta flames;
  zombies as backlit silhouettes with a red rim light that brightens as you close in. Critics split: ComicBook.com and CBR
  praise the warm, vivid palette against gothic architecture; Push Square called the art direction disappointing next to
  *Symphony of the Night*.
- **Movement and animation** are fast, fluid and responsive ("Dead Cells" heritage); enemy attacks are clearly telegraphed.
- **The whip** (the Vampire Killer) is the main attack *and* the traversal tool: swing, zip to rings, latch onto enemies to
  hurl yourself at them, cross large chasms and reach heights. It comes from a boss, and its traversal uses unlock as the
  game advances. One review (CBR) says latching can be unreliable.
- **Combat:** seven weapon classes (longsword, whip, gauntlets, spear, greatsword, sword and shield, twin swords), each with
  its own moveset, plus a parry. No sub-weapons from candles; powers are **Arcana**: defeating a boss seals it into a tarot
  card that grants a mana spell, upgraded through optional "Works of Mercy". Three relic slots; change loadout while paused.
- **Structure:** a large auto-filling map (castle and Paris), non-linear like *Symphony of the Night*; **bosses yield keys that
  open large doorways**; mirrors are save and fast-travel points; health comes from food, flasks and mirrors.
- **Story delivery worth noting:** defeated bosses are freed from Dracula's influence and speak in the hero's head: some
  supportive, some mocking. A boss's death becomes a voice that stays.
- **Reception:** generally favourable (Metacritic 78 Switch to 88 Xbox), 96% of critics recommending on OpenCritic.

## What to take
- The *feel* of a fast, buttery animation set with readable telegraphs, in a vivid, graphic, rim-lit look.
- A **traversal tool that is also a combat tool** (swing, zip, latch): the model for what the ninja finds.
- **Boss rewards that change what you can do**, and bosses that keep talking after they fall.
- A demo that is one whole chapter with its own boss, not a tech slice.

## What to be careful of
- **Reliability of the latch.** If the ninja's tool is the key to traversal, targeting must be dependable; a flaky grapple
  ruins both exploration and boss fights. Treat it as a first-class feel problem in Phase 2.
- **Vibe versus execution.** Critics disagree about whether the look lands. We are borrowing a *direction* (vibrant, graphic,
  fluid), not copying its models or style.
- **The pipeline.** A 3D-to-2D look is a different production method from drawing on a canvas. This is the main reason the
  art and tech decision (step 0.5) is open. Paths to weigh then: a 3D engine with toon shading; 3D models pre-rendered to 2D
  sprites (the Dead Cells approach); hand-drawn 2D with the same palette and motion; each with its cost in assets and tooling.
