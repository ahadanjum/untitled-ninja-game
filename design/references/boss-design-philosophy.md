# Boss design philosophy — Hollow Knight, Silksong, the Souls games, Ghost of Tsushima

Per the designer (D018): "Build them like Hollow Knight and Silksong bosses, with the design philosophy of those games as
well as the Souls games." This is a synthesis for the designer to react to, **not a rulebook yet**. Sources: the first
game's research (`../hollow-shinobi/design-research/01` §8 and `05` §1.10, §3), plus targeted searches on 2026-10-17. Items
marked *(secondhand)* come from a single report.

## What these games do (the pattern)
1. **A readable telegraph, then a punish window.** Every attack has a wind-up you can read (a pose, a voice bark, a line of
   light) and a recovery you can hit in: one to three hits in Hollow Knight, often tighter in Silksong.
2. **Two or three phases.** Later phases *add or upgrade moves and change the arena*; a scream, a stagger or a music change
   marks the turn. The cheapest, strongest escalation is an **overlay on known moves** (Silksong's Last Judge sets the same
   flail on fire).
3. **The arena is part of the fight.** Lava you can knock a boss into, a stage with fire from below, a ring that shrinks,
   platforms that fall away and turn the last phase into a climb.
4. **The boss is a character, and its theme becomes its move set.** Widow's bells, Trobbio's stage, Lace the manic fencer.
   Silksong's Phantom is a dark mirror of the hero; Lace returns across three acts and turns into an ally or a tragedy: *a
   relationship delivered through combat*. Defeated Mantis Lords **bow**: some fights end in respect, not death.
5. **A boss teaches the move it gives you.** Hornet's fight makes you want the dash she then gives; Phantom teaches the parry
   skill (Cross Stitch) you earn from it.
6. **Bosses are proactive in Silksong, reactive in Hollow Knight** *(secondhand, from Team Cherry's William Pellen)*: they
   chase and cut off your escape rather than only answering where you stand. Move sets are shared and extended: enemy
   soldiers reuse Hornet's boss moves.
7. **Music is part of the boss.** Each boss has its own track; phase two gets a faster variant (Team Cherry asked their composer
   for exactly that); silence is used before boss doors. No health bar in Hollow Knight: you read a boss's state from its
   behaviour and phase changes.
8. **Fewer, deeper bosses.** Humanoid bosses are duel partners; monsters test your tools and the arena. Optional bosses
   can have an optional way around them.
9. **Rest close to the boss.** Short runbacks; Silksong's long ones were a top complaint.

## What Ghost of Tsushima adds (for human opponents and the parry)
- **The glint language.** Blue = cannot be blocked: **parry or dodge**. Red = cannot be blocked *or* parried: **dodge or
  roll**. Our yellow and red (D010) are the same idea, with yellow in place of blue.
- **A predictable first attack followed by a flurry** (the combat lead's reasoning: matching the player's reaction speed
  made fights unreadable). A readable opener teaches the opponent's rhythm, then it breaks.
- **The Standoff** (inspired by the end of *Sanjuro*): two swordsmen face each other; you hold, and release the instant the
  other moves. Feints bait early releases. A duel can *begin* as a test of nerve.
- **Duels are personal before they are mechanical** (the praise for Ryuzo, and for Lord Shimura as the cleanest and most
  emotionally devastating duel). Shimura's duel removes your tools: only the sword.
- **Sekiro's version:** a duel is a conversation: the enemy says something with a move, you answer it.

## A caution from the first game
The designer called a boss that repeated one fast combo at the same speed in later phases "just spamming parries". The fix
those games use is the one above: later phases give you **new things to answer**, not the same thing faster.

## What this leaves open for 0.4 and Phase 1
Whether bosses show a health bar; how many phases the demo's two bosses have; what a phase change looks like for a human
opponent versus a demon; which of the two demo bosses is a duel (dialogue, standoff) and which tests the arena; what each
boss gives the hero.
