## The words we use
- **The compound:** the clan's home on the mountain. The demo's setting.
- **The attack (or "the night of the pact"):** the demon assault on the compound in Chapter 1, your original "insurgency." (I had been calling it
  "the siege"; I have dropped that word.)
- **The sanctum:** a hidden, forbidden hall in the oldest part of the compound where the **cursed blade is kept sealed**: its door is bound
  with rope and seals, the council keep the key, and the mother holds a spare. The place of the pact.
- **The lower halls:** the clan's secret halls beneath the compound: the cellar, the prison, the experiment rooms. Closed in Chapter 1, opened
  in Chapter 3.
- **Room, region:** a **room** is one space (a screen or a scrolling hall); a **region** (an *area* in Hollow Knight) is a cluster of many
  rooms with one identity, like Greenpath.

## The story in one paragraph
Kaname, a young shinobi raised by a clan that secretly built him, takes a cursed blade by pact to save his mother, his younger sibling and the
clan from a demonic attack on the compound. The clan, frightened by what he has become, exiles him. For years he tries to prove himself: he will
defeat the greatest demons of the exile lands and master his power, so that he will finally be accepted. Three rival clans hunt him, and
strange news from home pulls him back early, an outcast still. At home he learns what the clan did to him. The Elder, the mentor who was a
father to him, is killed before his eyes and tells him with his last breath that the council, calling them disloyal, has already sent his mother
and younger sibling into hell. Kaname rebels, and the council banishes him there too: *rot with them.* In hell a boss holds his mother and
sibling in two cages over lava and says only one can be saved. He refuses to choose and wins, and it changes nothing: his mother takes the choice
from him, gives him the truth about the world, and dies. He climbs out with his sibling. Three rival clans, each lying a little, tell him the
rest, and only then does he learn that the Elder is alive and is the evil demon behind it all, and that hell was where he was meant to grow
strong enough to be the Elder's body. The rival clans ask for his help to restore the balance. He returns to a clan the Elder has devoured,
defeats him, and makes the choice the story has been building to: let it go, or carry it.

## The founding secret (D058)
Centuries ago a family had a child touched by a demon. Three orders of shinobi each had an answer: the **Severing** would kill the
child; the **Wardens** would cage it; the **Open Hand** hid the family, then, pressed, gave up where they were. Hunted by all three,
the parents made a pact with a demon to keep their child alive. The demon kept the bargain, and kept the child: he became the demon's
first vessel, and failed, and became **the Gatekeeper**. The family became the clan; the demon became its patriarch, the "godfather"
who gave it its arts and asked for vessels in return. Each generation the clan made vessels from children; the failures became **the
made**, the demons. A vessel must say *yes* (it is not taken by force), so the clan engineered every circumstance that might make a
child say it. **Kaname is the first to be strong enough, and willing enough, to succeed.**
- It is **made versus chosen** at the scale of a bloodline: love, a pact and a price, over and over.
- **The clan's pact and Kaname's are the same act for the same reason.** The difference is who pays: he pays with himself; the clan paid
  with other people's children.
- **Nobody was innocent.** Each rival clan failed the first child and has hidden its part since. That is why each can only give a
  *fragment* and cannot say the rest (D044).

**The exile, earned (Chapter 1).** (1) The law is shown first: the creed that a shinobi who bargains with demons is cast out. (2) The player
has felt the fear: in the main fight the blade's hunger moves the arm, and afterwards **his sibling, who has always supported him, flinches,
once, involuntarily, and reaches for him at once, ashamed** (confirmed, D069). That one flinch is why he goes. (3) The family's words and
faces disagree: the father's first question is about the blade, and he alone does not flinch at the arm; the mother is silent; the Elder
protests. (4) **He chooses to go**, because he protects the people close to him: made versus chosen in miniature, and exactly what the Elder
wanted. **The clan sets him no terms** (D073); the goal he chases in Chapter 2 is entirely his own.

## Hell (D061, as amended by D067, D068, D070, D072)
- **How he gets there.** He has no way into hell, and no wish to be sent; the council give him one, as a punishment. Having fought them, he is
  banished through the demon-gate: **"We're sending you there so you can rot with them."** He falls, shown, not described. It is a punishment,
  and it is what he needed.
- **Why his family was sent.** The council sent his mother and sibling for no longer acting in line with the clan (they took his side; the
  mother had given him the key and questioned the tonic). That is all the council know. Much later it turns out to have been the Elder's plot to
  make Kaname stronger, which is why the Elder's last words tell him where they are.
- **Shape.** The made's world lies below the compound; the keep is at the bottom; the climb out is a long ascent past the entry point and out
  through a new passage into the Open Hand's land.
- **Its wants.** (1) **Reach his family.** (2) **Kin:** the made are people the clan turned into monsters, and some are dissenters the clan has
  caged here for years; to them Kaname is *one who got out.* A safe place with its own music and small warmth (D016). (3) **The temptation:**
  the pact-demons' voices say it will stop hurting if he submits.
- **The Gatekeeper.** The first child. He holds Kaname's mother and sibling in two cages suspended over a pathway above lava (**open one and the
  other falls**) and tells him *only one can be saved*. He recognises Kaname ("we both said yes to save someone") and hums the lullaby.
- **A scene with no choice for the player (D068).** Kaname **refuses**: he will save both. He fights, and defeats the Gatekeeper, and **nothing
  changes**: the rule of the cages is older than the Gatekeeper, and opening one still drops the other. He cannot choose. **His mother takes the
  choice from him.** She says: **"Take your sibling. I'm sorry for what's happened to you. Here's the truth."** She lets her own cage fall, which lowers her child's to the path, and she dies; he takes his sibling
  out of hell. Her fate is fixed.
- **The truth she gives (D070).** What she learned from the caged dissenters: the demons are people, made from children; the clan serves a
  **Patron**, a demon whose face no one has ever seen; the council believe it is making a weapon; and Kaname was made for a purpose she half-
  understands, to be a body. She **does not know the Patron is the Elder.** The player will suspect, and will have been shown that the Elder died.
- **No voice from the dark.** There is no Elder reveal here; it comes at the end of Chapter 5.

## The curse's price and gift (D062)
- **The blade is a pure reward in play.** The price is *what it does to him*: each essence is a wordless beat of revulsion, resignation, or
  ease; the markings on his right forearm deepen and never spread past it; people's reactions follow his deeds, not his scar.
- **Essence is mercy.** In hell he learns the demons are people and that the made *want* him to take their essence because it ends their pain.
  The mechanic he hates is the thing that can free and carry them. Every essence in Chapters 1–3 is reread.
- **The Elder preaches acceptance.** "Stop hating yourself. Let it in." From him that is grooming: he needs the vessel to say yes.
  The world's acceptance (the sibling, the healer, the ferryman, the made) is unconditional and small. **Kaname's real acceptance is defiance:
  he keeps what he is and refuses what he was made for.**

## The Elder (D060, D070, D074)
- **His motive (D074).** He loves the family he built, a real and possessive love, **and he wants to bring more demonic essence into the world and
  change its balance. He should feel evil.** The player should come to hate what he is while understanding what he loves.
- **At home on the night of the attack** because he is useless in a fight and invaluable in a crisis: calming the first time, chilling the second.
- **Kind and useful throughout.** He protests the exile, gives the rope, teaches in **flashbacks** at rest points (a short playable memory of him
  teaching the move Kaname is about to need), and guides through **paper cranes** left at rest points in Chapters 2–5. The council are the suspects.
- **He feeds the guilt, then offers relief:** "Stop hating yourself. Let it in."
- **His death is shown** at the midpoint. His last crane is the first the player will recognise, so the cranes first read as love ("they were
  him"), and after Chapter 5 they read as control.
- **The exile and the descent were his design.** The council think they banished a traitor; he made sure Kaname went where he would grow.
- **Layers of reveal:** the Patron is older than the clan (fragments, Chapters 2 and 4); **the Patron is the Elder and alive (end of Chapter 5);**
  his purpose, confirmed to Kaname's face (Chapter 6).

## The lullaby (D076)
The lullaby is a lock. The clan sang it to every vessel to open the mind to the Patron's voice. **Kaname's mother, suspecting, changed one note**
when she sang it to him. That is why the grooming never fully took, and why he can refuse: his choice was made possible by his mother's small act
of love. The Gatekeeper and the made hum the original (a note off, a fair clue). In Chapter 6 the Elder hums the original to coax his yes, and it
does not work.

## The council (D063)
They believe they are making a **weapon**; the Elder is making a **body**. They wear masks in public; each defeat breaks a mask. Most fights are at
the midpoint, where they are strongest and strangest; by Chapter 6 the Elder has used them up. **His final fight wears their powers**: phases that
reuse moves of the council bosses the player already beat, a graduation exam of the whole story.

## The ending (D064, as amended by D070): Let Go or Carry
After the Elder falls, he is at Kaname's feet, and the last seals are almost burned. Like Ghost of Tsushima's choice about Shimura, both
options are right and neither is free. **The mother's death was a choice taken from him; this is the one that is his.**
- **Let Go: kill him.** The seals burn out, the pact ends, the curse lifts: the scar fades, the essences fade, the made rest and their voices go
  quiet. The blade stays as plain steel. He is a shinobi again, with only what he was *taught*. *Peace, and loss.* The people see a man.
- **Carry: take him in.** He absorbs the Elder into the curse, with his hunger to bring more of his kind into the world. The seals burn on
  Kaname's terms, with no master and no leash. The chorus of the freed swells; the Elder's voice remains inside him. He becomes a power no one
  controls. *Company, and vigilance.* The people see a hopeful spirit, or something to fear, and the epilogue holds both.
In both, **he refuses what they made him for** (D029); the choice is how to hold what is left. Both are always open to the player; earlier
choices, such as optional essences, colour the epilogue and lock nothing.

## The attack, explained (D077)
Word of a raid reaches the elders, so the council and the clan's men go to meet it. The raid is the pretext, and their absence lets the
demon-gate in the lower halls be opened and the rituals performed (by the Elder, who stayed behind). The made come up through the mountain's
roots, so from the compound it looks like an assault from the forest. Kaname, left with the old, the young, the injured and a few guards, is
there to try to stop it, and the rest of the story follows from that night. The raid was a rumour about the Severing, which also gives the
present-day feud with them a cause.
