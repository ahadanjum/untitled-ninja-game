# World sketch — how the whole game interconnects (step 1.1, first pass)

You noticed the story "feels and sounds a little more linear" than Hollow Knight or Castlevania. This is a first answer, all
PROPOSED (P19, P20). Nothing here is a room plan. Regions are placeholders until the story is final.

## The honest diagnosis
The *story* in the pitch is linear: exile, journey, home, hell, rivals, reckoning. That is fine: most good metroidvanias have a
story order. What matters is that the **map** is not a line. Hollow Knight tells its story out of order because you pick
the order; Dark Souls and Castlevania: Symphony of the Night keep a story order inside a world that loops back on itself. We can
do the second: a staged story in a connected world, with the stages as **events and chapters, not corridors**.

## The idea: home is the hub, and you visit it three times
- **The clan's mountain is the world's centre.** It is the first area (the demo), the midpoint (the homecoming and the fall into
  hell) and the last (the reckoning). Each visit is a different place: the first at night under attack, the second in daylight
  with the clan back in power and doors you could not open before, the third in ruin or fire.
- **Most of the mountain is closed in Chapter 1** on purpose: the council hall, the lower halls (cellar, prison,
  experiment rooms) and the way down to the demon-gate. (The sanctum where the blade lies is open for the pact, then sealed again.) With the whip-like tool and the movement unlocks you earn on the road, you open them on the way back. This is exactly
  the backtracking you described: "now you have new tools to journey to new areas in your home."
- **Shortcuts lead home.** From far regions a back way leads into the compound (a pilgrim's stair, a drainage tunnel, a smuggler's
  rope bridge), opened from the far side, so home is close to everything once you know the ways. This is Hollow Knight's
  Dirtmouth and Crossroads logic: the hub is a place you keep returning to.

## Hell is beneath home (P20)
- **Entering:** the council banish him through the demon-gate beneath the compound (D067): "rot with them." A long vertical fall, shown,
  not described.
- **Shape:** a **dark mirror of the mountain**: the clan's halls above, the demon-making halls below that echo them, as Symphony
  of the Night's inverted castle echoes the castle. The climb out is a reverse tour of "what was under your feet", a long
  vertical region, a different dimension that is still physically under the map.
- **Leaving:** he climbs out with his sibling (D061), and the way up emerges somewhere *new*: the **Open Hand's land**. That creates a new connection across the map; after
  the story it is a shortcut and a loop, and optional hell content can be reached from the rival side.

## The world changes as the story changes
- **Rival territory flips.** Before the truth, its shinobi hunt you; after, they bow and no longer fight (like the Mantis Lords'
  village), and they help against the demons that rise from the ground or through portals. The same rooms, different life.
- **The compound changes state** at each visit (attack, daylight, ruin). **NPCs move**; the road's regions can fill with
  new enemies or new friends as chapters pass.
- **Rule:** a state change must reward the player for coming back: a new shortcut, a new door, a line of dialogue, a gift.

## Techniques to keep it interconnected (the Purpose Rule applies to each)
1. Every region has at least two ways in, and no region is a dead-end spur unless the dead end is the point (a boss, a secret).
2. Locks are seen before their keys; keys sit far from their locks.
3. A shortcut opens from the far side and is foreshadowed (seen, unreachable).
4. Vertical stacking: places on different layers sit above and below each other so the player *feels* the map.
5. A chapter's gate is a story event (a boss, a reveal), not a wall of rooms. Within a chapter the order is the player's.
6. A recurring melody or landmark (the home lullaby, the mountain on the skyline) tells the player where they are in the story.

## The Road of Exile (Chapter 2): your answer, made concrete (D047)
You described it right: not one giant room, but **two or three regions**, each a cluster of many rooms, like Greenpath or the Fungal
Wastes. My first pass (D059, working names):
- **Three regions, one per rival clan** (the Severing, the Wardens, the Open Hand), set around a **crossroads, the Road itself**, which
  is the hub of Chapter 2 in the way Hollow Knight's Forgotten Crossroads is. Each region is reachable two ways, so the order is the
  player's, and each is its own place with its own music (D008) and its own leader to fight.
- **Gating inside the chapter:** the traversal tool matures here (new uses, not a new tool), and a second movement unlock (wall cling or
  a double jump) opens a closed route in each region, so the player returns to places they have already seen.
- **Each region holds a fragment of the truth** and a hidden way that leads *toward home*, so by the end of the chapter the player knows
  three routes back to the mountain, which Chapter 3 will use.
- **The demon boss that ends the chapter** lives where the three regions meet, so the player must have seen all of them first. Kaname then heads home early (D073); the regions stay open, and what he left undone he can return to.
